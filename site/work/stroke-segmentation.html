Stroke Lesion Segmentation on MRI

Five models, two datasets, and a simpler framework that performed best.

Role
Research Assistant · Qatar University
Stack
PyTorch · DAE-Former · LKA · DLKA · FCT · nnUNet
Year
2024
Venue
IEEE Access
Status
Published

The problem. Stroke lesions on MRI are small, irregular, and heavily outnumbered by healthy tissue. Severe class imbalance is the kind of setting where more elaborate architectures are supposed to earn their keep.

The approach. Compared DAE-Former, LKA, DLKA, FCT, and nnUNet on two public stroke datasets, covering Transformer, attention-based CNN, hybrid, and self-adaptive segmentation designs.

nnUNet achieved the strongest results in this study. Architecture complexity alone did not determine performance.

What the comparison tests. Different model families respond differently to lesion variability. The paper also examines how preprocessing and postprocessing influence segmentation quality.

The result. The study reports that nnUNet outperformed the more complex alternatives across the evaluated datasets.

What this changes. Controlled benchmarking as a default. It raises the bar for any "novel architecture" claim that hasn't actually held the rest of the pipeline fixed.

Zafari-Ghadim, Soliman, Yousif, Ibrahim, Rashed, Mabrok. "Deep Models for Stroke Segmentation: Do Complex Architectures Always Perform Better?" IEEE Access, 2024.Read the paper ↗
