D3 — A Small Language Model for Drug–Drug Interaction Prediction

A ~70M-parameter specialist matches billion-parameter LLMs at a fraction of the inference cost.

Role
AI Research Specialist · WCM-Q
Stack
Python · PyTorch · Hugging Face · Pretraining · SFT
Year
2025
Venue
Machine Learning with Applications · Elsevier
Status
Published

The problem. Drug–Drug Interactions are a leading cause of preventable adverse events, and predicting them is exactly the kind of narrow, well-bounded task where deploying a billion-parameter general-purpose LLM is an expensive answer.

The approach. A compact ~70M-parameter small language model, pretrained and fine-tuned on biomedical interaction data for DDI prediction. The bet is simple: a small specialist trained on the right data can keep up with a generalist that's much larger.

The 70M specialist matched billion-parameter LLMs — at a fraction of the inference cost.

What was hard. A fair benchmark. Comparing a small specialist to a billion-parameter LLM only means something if the evaluation, the splits, and the metric definitions are the same on both sides.

What shipped. A peer-reviewed comparison in Machine Learning with Applications (Elsevier, 2025) showing the small specialized model reaches comparable accuracy at a fraction of the inference cost.

What this changes. For narrow prediction tasks at scale, a small specialist with clean training and evaluation discipline is often the more deployable answer than a billion-parameter generalist.

Published in Machine Learning with Applications, Elsevier, 2025.
