The problem. Three different clinical questions tend to need three different tools — asking the record, summarizing a report, and finding the patient like this one. Most assistants split each into its own pipeline, with its own retrieval, prompts, and evaluation. And plain LLMs hallucinate, age badly, and can't explain themselves — disqualifying in medicine.

The approach. One Retrieval-Augmented Generation pipeline behind one domain-specific retriever, serving all three tasks. Privacy came first: the training corpus was generated synthetically with Mistral v0.3, Qwen 2.5, and Llama 3 — no real patient ever enters training — and the system was then validated on de-identified MIMIC-IV-Note records.

Correctness 0.91, groundedness 0.89, retrieval relevance 0.92 — and every answer traces back into the record.

What was hard. Evaluation. MERA is scored across complementary signals — correctness, relevance, groundedness, and retrieval relevance for QA; ROUGE-1 F1 and Jaccard for summaries; METEOR for similarity — so a model that shines on one axis still has to hold up on the others, and results had to stay consistent when moving from synthetic charts to real EHRs.

What shipped. A peer-reviewed assistant in Machine Learning and Knowledge Extraction — to our knowledge the first to unify clinical question answering, summarization, and patient-case similarity in a single RAG framework.

What this changes. A reusable template for clinical RAG: one retriever, one evaluation harness, three tools behind it — and a privacy recipe (synthetic training, de-identified validation) that other hospitals can copy.
